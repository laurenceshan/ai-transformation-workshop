# Financial Baseline — Stage 0 Guide

The first thing this workshop does is help the user read their own P&L
(profit & loss statement / 损益表) clearly: is revenue constrained, or are
costs too high? Every later number hangs off this baseline. Read this file
before starting Stage 0.

## Setup Questions (ask at the very start, before anything else)

Three confirmations, one batch:

1. **Role & scope** — "Are we looking at the whole company (owner / investor /
   C-suite) or a single department (department head)?" This determines which
   P&L applies: company-wide vs departmental. Never mix the two scopes in one
   analysis.
2. **Precision mode** — "Do you want rigorous quantification, or a rough
   estimate?" Rigorous mode: per-item confirmation + three-tier ROI ranges.
   Rough mode: range estimates allowed, BUT the number-confirmation
   requirement is NOT relaxed — key figures still come from the user.
3. **Attachments** — invite upload of any financial material: financial
   statements, a listed company's annual report, a business ledger, even
   household bills for a side business. Returning users: also invite the
   previous workshop report as an input (see "Returning Users" below).

## Decomposing the P&L

From the uploaded material (or from Stage 1 Q&A when no attachment exists),
extract a simplified P&L:

- **Revenue side**: revenue lines, price x volume per line, growth/decline
  trend.
- **Cost side**: split by category — typically labor (人员), external spend
  (采购/外包/投放), capital/investment (资本开支/折旧), other overhead.
- **Deliverable of Stage 0**: a confirmed one-screen baseline table, plus the
  verdict sentence: "Your constraint is primarily on the revenue side" or
  "...on the cost side" (or mixed — say so).

Every extracted figure is shown to the user for confirmation before use.
If the user's documents are incomplete, mark gaps explicitly instead of
filling them with assumptions.

## Cost-Structure-Adaptive Questioning

Let the dominant cost category shape your questions:

| If costs are mostly... | Ask about... |
|---|---|
| Labor (人员高) | headcount, hours per task, loaded hourly rates, overtime, attrition |
| External spend (外部支出高) | procurement prices, vendor contracts, ad spend efficiency, renewal cycles |
| Capital/investment (投资高) | depreciation schedules, utilization of assets, payback expectations |
| Mixed/unclear | rank the top 3 cost lines first, then drill the largest |

The same applies to revenue: subscription → ask churn and conversion;
project-based → ask win rate and project margin; transaction-based → ask
volume and take rate.

## Returning Users (importing a previous report)

A user may upload a report from a previous workshop. Use it to skip
rediscovery — but NEVER trust it blindly, because the previous numbers are
often exactly what needs correcting:

**Diff-first verification.** Compare the old report against what the user
says now, and only re-ask three classes of items:

1. **Stale assumptions** — anything time-sensitive (prices, headcount,
   volumes) that may have changed since the last report.
2. **Missing data** — gaps the previous round marked EST or left open.
3. **User-flagged corrections** — items the user now says were wrong.

Everything else is marked "confirmed as-is" and reused. Walk the five stages
in order regardless — the old report accelerates each stage, it does not
skip it.
